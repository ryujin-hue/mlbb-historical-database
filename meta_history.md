# MLBB Meta History — Ten Years of the Land of Dawn (2016–2026)

This narrative reconstructs how the Mobile Legends: Bang Bang meta evolved era by era, from launch (2016) to patch 2.2.16 (October 2026). It is synthesized from the patch index, hero/item change logs, verified system milestones, M-Series pick/ban records, and sourced analyst tier summaries. Because the 2016–2019 patch archive was only partially reachable (see `research_report.md` §5), the early-era sections lean on cross-verified milestones and are explicitly conservative; counts of buffs/nerfs cited below reflect the 350 captured change rows and are therefore a floor, not a census.

![Project NEXT](images/objective/onees_project_next.jpg)

## 1. The Origin Era (2016–2017) — raw stats and the first systems

MLBB launched on Android (July 14, 2016) and iOS (November 9, 2016) with roughly ten heroes and grew to about 30 within the year (Miya, Layla, Tigreal, Alucard, Nana, Fanny, Franco and the rest of the classic roster — 33 heroes dated or reported 2016 in our master table). The early meta was defined by simple stat sticks: items were fewer and more linear (the original equipment set, with early renames such as Demon Hunter Sword at 1.1.85), emblems were plain stat pages, and the battle-spell kit was different from today's — Fury, Interference and Stun were remade into Inspire, Aegis and Petrify at 1.1.86, and Retribution's cooldown sat at 30s before a 35s standardization (1.2.46). Patch 1.2.16 (2017-09-15) brought the first landmark gameplay rework (Akai), and 1.2.14 reworked Turtle, Lord, jungle and brush timings — the first evidence that objectives, not just hero stats, would steer the meta. Draft was shallow (few bans), so the era's dominant picks were simply the strongest stat templates; the game's competitive identity barely existed yet, with the Riot lawsuit (2017) forcing the name change to "Mobile Legends: Bang Bang" the same year the first regional structures began forming.

## 2. The Expansion Era (2018–2019) — MPL, draft depth, and the road to Project NEXT

By November 2018 the roster hit 70 heroes (Wikipedia-verified), and the esports skeleton matured: MSC's first editions (2017 Jakarta, 2018 Jakarta) and the launch of MPL Philippines and MPL Indonesia (2018) created the professional circuit that would define MLBB's meta history. Patch-wise the era saw the Celestial Palace map revamp (1.3.36) and the first true balance discipline appearing in notes — our captured rows include Guinevere (1.3.46) and Dyrroth (1.3.88) adjustments and Bruno's 2019 revamp (1.3.96/1.3.98 Advanced). Late 2019, Moonton previewed the assassin rework wave (Natalia, Hanzo, Lancelot teased at 1.4.60) and, more importantly, announced **Project NEXT**: a sweeping program of hero visual/gameplay revamps, tutorial overhaul, and map modernization. The draft system expanded to three bans per side around 2019–2020 (verified chain in `systems.csv`), which made specialist picks viable and ended the pure "pick two OP heroes" era. In our captured dataset the earliest heavily-touched veterans (Argus with 4 buffs, Estes with 3) date from this stretch — the beginning of the long balance treadmill that still runs today.

## 3. Project NEXT and the Pandemic Boom (2020–2021) — M2, rework wave, Yu Zhong and Mathilda era

Project NEXT phased in from 2020 (verifiable phase dates 2020-09-22, 2021-06-15, 2021-09-21), revamping legacy heroes and the tutorial while the pandemic pushed viewership to record levels: M2 (January 2021) peaked at 3.08M viewers with Bren Esports' KarlTzy crowned FMVP, and M3 (December 2021) hit 3.19M as Blacklist International popularized the **utility/support-centric "BLCK" meta** — a draft philosophy built around set-up heroes, roam itemization and objective control rather than raw damage. Our hero-change log is densest from here: Yu Zhong (1.4.86, 2020-06-19), Khaleed, Barats, Popol and Kupa, Beatrix (3 nerfs captured in her first year — the fastest nerf bat we recorded), Phoveus, Valentina, Aamon, Edith, Melissa, Xavier, Julian and Fredrinn all arrived in this window. Systemically this was the era of the big 1.5.88 reset (2021): legacy jungle and roam equipment (Courage Mask line) was deleted in favor of the roaming-footwear + blessing model and Retribution upgrades, a structural change that standardized the jungle/roam economy every later patch balanced against. The emblem system, however, still ran on the legacy seven-page stat model — its replacement was already being tested on the Advanced Server by ~April 2022.

## 4. The Emblem Revolution and the Filipino Golden Age (2022–2023) — M4, M5, 1.7.44

Two dates anchor this era: **1.6.42 (announced 2021-12-30, live January 2022)** replaced the Celestial/Imperial map with Sanctum Island, and **1.7.44 (2022-12-20)** shipped the emblem revamp — seven role sets with Standard (16) and Core (8) talent pools, pick-3 loadouts — alongside the Swift Crossbow addition. Emblems stopped being passive stat pages and became per-match draft decisions, which sharpened counter-picking at every level of play. Esports-wise the Philippines took total control: ECHO won M4 in Bali (Bennyqt FMVP, 4.27M peak viewers), then AP Bren won M5 in Manila (FlapTzy FMVP, 5.07M peak — then the all-time record) after winning MSC 2023, completing the rare "double crown." Item design also modernized with the 2023 "NEXT" items (Starlium Scythe, Malefic Gun) whose execute/scaling passives pushed late-game power ceilings — an early marker of the power-creep debate that dominates 2024–2026 discussions. On the ladder, Season 28 (2023-03-25) replaced Mythic Points with the star system and Mythical Honor tier, and Season 29 added Mythical Immortal, giving the ranked ladder its modern shape.

## 5. The Modern Era (2024–2026) — 2.x, the 10-ban draft, and Aurora's M7

January 2024 brought the last major draft change we could verify: **10 bans (5 per side) in Mythic+**, converting high-rank drafts into full information wars. 2024 was Fnatic ONIC Philippines' year (M6 champions in Kuala Lumpur, Kelra FMVP, 4.02M peak), 2024 also added Sky Piercer and Broken Heart (execute and drain passives that nerfed-then-rebalanced repeatedly), Twilight Armor was removed (2.1.30) with Chastise Pauldron replacing it, and MSC moved permanently under the Esports World Cup (2024 Riyadh, 2025 Riyadh, 2026 Paris — Team Spirit taking the 2026 title 4–3 over Yangon Galacticos, the first western MLBB international crown). The version scheme jumped to **2.x in late 2025** (1.9.99 → 2.1.02 with no 1.10.x/2.0.x in between), and the official 2.1.40 notes (2025-12-25) — which we mined in full — show the modern balance granularity: e.g. Zhuxin's mana regen per butterfly cut from 5% to 3% while her Soul Snare base damage dropped 300–800 to 200–300 (+10–15% target Max HP), an example of the flat-to-percent conversion philosophy of current balancing. M7 (January 2026, Jakarta) ended with **Aurora Gaming PH sweeping Alter Ego 4–0**, Light as FMVP, and a 5.68M peak-viewer record; the 2026 Annual Map Change (2026-09-28) added Revealing Wisps, Golden Turrets and a Healing Turtle, and Brawl was fully revamped (2026-06-17). Ranked Season 42 — the "Starward Decade" 10th-anniversary season — runs to 2026-12-16.

## 7. Power Creep and the M-Series Meta vs Ranked Meta

Power creep in MLBB is structural rather than scalar: newer kits bundle mobility + CC + damage + utility in ways 2016 kits never did (compare Hirara's 2026 kit against launch-roster stat templates), and the item/emblem systems amplify it — execute passives (Sky Piercer), percent-HP damage (Zhuxin's reworked Soul Snare), and pick-3 talent loadouts all raise the ceiling of what a "balanced" hero must do. Moonton's counter-strategy has been systemic: the 10-ban draft dilutes any single OP pick, roam/jungle income reworks (1.5.96, 1.6.50, 2.1.40, 2.1.88 chains) tax snowballing, and the annual map changes (Sanctum 2022, NEXT 2024, 2026 Wisps/Turrets) re-randomize objective priorities faster than pure number-tuning could.

The M-Series meta has consistently diverged from the ranked meta in three ways. First, **draft speed**: pro games settle flex picks and counters within 5 bans, while ranked Mythic+ drafts resolve around comfort picks — so heroes like Kaja, Mathilda and set-up tanks dominate pro pick rates while remaining mid-tier in pubs. Second, **objective math**: pro metas form around Lord/Turtle timing windows (e.g. the S23 Lord changes — 8:00/3:00 spawns with 18:00 evolution — reshaped M-series macro before pub ladders absorbed them), which is why utility compositions (Blacklist M3, AP Bren M5) outlive burst comps at the highest level. Third, **patch freeze**: international events play on frozen patches (only MSC 2024=1.8.92 and MSC 2025=1.9.91 could be verified in this run — the rest are flagged `UNVERIFIED`), so each M-Series memorializes one meta snapshot; M7's 5.68M-viewer peak came on a patch whose Aurora-vs-Alter Ego grand final showcased the 2025-2026 assassin/roam-item economy at its most refined.

## 8. What to Watch Next

Three open threads close the meta story as of October 2026. The 2026 Annual Map Change (September 28) is still being digested by the ladder — Healing Turtle in particular may re-weight early-macro compositions heading into M8. The post-Hirara hero slot is officially unannounced (our `Upcoming` rows list what official channels have teased), so the 134th hero will be the first release of the Savvy Games Group era — with ownership changing hands in 2026, monetization pressure (Collector/Starlight cadence) and esports investment (M8 Türkiye, January 2027) are the two dials to watch. And the 10th-anniversary "ALL IN" campaign (October 2026, 10 countries) marks the transition from ByteDance-era MLBB to whatever the next decade of the Land of Dawn becomes.
